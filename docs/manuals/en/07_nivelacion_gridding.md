To opt in explicitly in a dialog, a model or a batch, enable **Allow large minimum-curvature grids** in Advanced parameters. Large grids can require substantially more memory and time.

## Processing order

Levelling seeks to reduce discrepancies between lines after temporal and acquisition corrections. Gridding estimates a field on a lattice; it does not itself fix lag, base-station or height errors. Review line geometry, crossover quality and network connectivity first.

A crossover difference between comparable observations constrains relative corrections. A disconnected or unanchored network does not determine an absolute datum. Robust constant or polynomial fits reduce the influence of some residuals, but degree and accepted lines remain user choices. Record rejected crossovers, fitted corrections and residuals per line.

## Quantitative interpolation validation

On a synthetic flight survey with five million observations on 100 lines, the 159,197-cell grid at 25 m yielded an RMS of 0.000355 for minimum curvature with tension 0.1 and 0.000282 for bidirectional PCHIP, in analytic field units. Computation took 35.2 and 2.25 s respectively, with process memory peaks of 412.5 and 389.7 MiB. The experiment used regular geometry and in-memory inputs and excluded raster export. Cross-line error still varied by position between lines.

The multilevel solver uses a default limit of 500,000 working nodes, including margins. A 50 km square at 25 m requires 4,020,025 nodes. The program reports the limit and suggests a cell size of at least 71.5 m for this extent. External comparison results await clearance for disclosure. [Briggs (1974)](https://doi.org/10.1190/1.1440410) · [Smith and Wessel (1990)](https://doi.org/10.1190/1.1442837)

## Choosing an interpolator

| Method | Main controls | Precaution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nearest neighbour | Point distribution | Discontinuities between neighbour regions |
| IDW | Power, neighbours, radius | Dense lines may dominate |
| Global TPS | Smoothness and full support | Dense-system cost |
| Local TPS | Neighbourhood and smoothness | Not equivalent to the global fit |
| Experimental minimum curvature | Grid, iterations and convergence | Check residuals and limits; do not assume proprietary equivalence |
| Sinc | Sampling and support | Cannot recover unmeasured detail |
| Ordinary kriging | Variogram, neighbours, nugget | Statistical model needs justification |
| Equivalent sources | Source depth and damping | Regularised physical problem, not neutral interpolation |

Smaller cells may improve display without increasing physical resolution. Fix extent, origin and cell size before comparing products. Numerical comparisons require the same lattice or documented resampling.

## Flight-line gridding

Open **Grid survey points to GeoTIFF** in Processing. Select a numeric channel, a projected CRS and, for bidirectional gridding, the production flight-line ID field. Exclude tie lines, turns and lines with incompatible headings. With line IDs and automatic cell size, the median cross-line spacing is divided by 4 or 5. Without line IDs, automatic sizing uses point density.

Bidirectional gridding interpolates linearly along each profile, then linearly or with PCHIP across profiles. Optional detrending fits a plane, interpolates residuals and restores the plane. Along-line and cross-line gap limits prevent bridging unsupported intervals. For oblique lines, the flight-aligned grid is resampled on the projected CRS axes.

Tensioned minimum curvature uses off-node Briggs constraints, coarse-to-fine refinement and discrete natural edge conditions. Inspect the GeoTIFF convergence metadata. Disable cell averaging to select the closest observation to each node directly at each level when along-line sampling is dense.

Null and non-finite values are excluded. Supply a numeric dummy value if the channel uses a finite sentinel. Combine a maximum distance with the **survey_boundary** polygon generated by the survey map to assign NoData outside coverage. The polygon preserves islands and holes and is transformed to the output CRS. Distance is measured from the cell center to valid observations.

## Spatial validation

Reserve blocks or lines before selecting interpolator parameters. Measure errors at unused points and examine their locations; a global RMS can conceal poor edge extrapolation. Random validation between nearby points on one line can be overly favourable.

An empirical variogram depends on distance, bins, trend, available pairs and anisotropy. Its model does not make kriging variance the total acquisition or interpretation uncertainty. Equivalent sources require separation from receivers, explicit units and a defined prediction surface.

## Microlevelling

Use it after primary corrections and crossover levelling to treat residual directional corrugation. Compare the removed correction with the amplitude and orientation of real structures. Changing azimuth, cutoff or mask can alter geological signal, particularly along flight-parallel structures.

Preserve line corrections, the applied surface and final product separately. A smoother appearance is not a sufficient acceptance criterion.
